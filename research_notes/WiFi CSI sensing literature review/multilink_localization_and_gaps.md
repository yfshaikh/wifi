# Multi-link device-free localization and zone/occupancy sensing with low-cost radio networks: prior work and gaps for a 1-TX x N-RX ESP32-C6 CSI network

Scope note: this covers RSS-based RTI/DFL, CSI-based multi-link localization, scaling and link-selection studies, unsynchronized multi-receiver fusion, and candidate original contributions for RIDE Fall 2026 Group 6 (7x ESP32-C6, 1 ESP-NOW broadcaster at 100 Hz, amplitude-only CSI, MQTT to a laptop hub, no clock sync). Research budget was about 20 search/fetch calls. Only one full text was read directly: the Utah-hosted RTI preprint. Everything else is verified from bibliographic records (dblp, IEEE/ACM DOIs, arXiv, ISC indexes) and abstracts. Numbers taken only from abstracts or aggregators are flagged as such.

## (a) Radio tomographic imaging (RTI) and RSS-based device-free localization

### Takeaway
RSS-based DFL began with Youssef et al. (MobiCom 2007). The Utah group (Wilson, Patwari, Kaltiokallio, Bocca, Zhao) then formalized it as RTI. Their testbeds were 2.4 GHz 802.15.4 nodes in dense peer-to-peer meshes of 20-34 nodes, using every node pair (N(N-1)/2 links). Later work moved from shadowing-based imaging to variance-based imaging (for motion), skew-Laplace statistical models, kernel/histogram distances (for stationary people), and multi-channel "fade level" weighting. The whole line assumes a mesh of many links, not a single broadcaster heard by a few receivers.

### Cited Findings
- **Youssef, Mah, Agrawala, "Challenges: Device-free passive localization for wireless environments," ACM MobiCom 2007, pp. 222-229 (Montreal, Sept 9-14, 2007).** Introduces "device-free passive" (DfP) localization: detecting, tracking and identifying entities that carry no device. It shows feasibility with ordinary Wi-Fi equipment. Applications include intrusion detection and perimeter protection. — [Paper copy (U. Crete reading list)](https://www.csd.uoc.gr/~hy439/reading/list_2011/10.1.1.112.6849.pdf); [Paper Digest record](https://www.paperdigest.org/certificate/a85d62fbcb17e530)
- Follow-up from the same group: **Nuzzer** (large-scale DfP localization) — [arXiv 0908.0893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0908.0893). The node count and accuracy of Nuzzer were not extracted in this session.
- **Wilson & Patwari, "Radio Tomographic Imaging with Wireless Networks," IEEE Trans. Mobile Computing 9(5):621-632, 2010, DOI 10.1109/TMC.2009.174.** — [Scilit record](https://scilit.com/publications/d4427f86b28a99d6096a6c1de0be37f0); [Utah PDF](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)
  - Linear model: RSS attenuation on each link is a weighted sum of voxel attenuations. Weights use an ellipse model: a pixel gets nonzero weight if it lies inside an ellipse with the link endpoints as foci, normalized by 1/sqrt(link length). Reconstruction uses regularized (Tikhonov/MAP) inversion. — [Utah PDF](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)
  - Experiment: "twenty eight 'Telosb' wireless nodes by Crossbow (2.4 GHz)" in a square 14 ft per side (196 sq ft), eight nodes per side at 2 ft spacing. With K nodes, every node pair counts as a link. — [Utah PDF](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)
  - The authors claim imaging "power that increases as Metcalf's Law with the number of nodes" and argue for many low-complexity devices. — [Utah PDF](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)
  - Outdoors, the same number of nodes gave the same image "quality" over a larger area, which the authors attribute to less multipath. — [Utah PDF](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)
  - CAVEAT: the Utah-hosted PDF is a 2008 preprint (PDF metadata dated 2008-08-25). The published TMC version may report different numbers. That preprint gives no quantitative localization error and no sampling rate.
- **Wilson & Patwari, "See-Through Walls: Motion Tracking Using Variance-Based Radio Tomography Networks," IEEE Trans. Mobile Computing 10(5):612-621, May 2011, DOI 10.1109/TMC.2010.175.** The arXiv preprint has a different title: "Through-Wall Motion Tracking Using Variance-Based Radio Tomography Networks," arXiv 0909.5417. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5417); [ISC record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/1330912.htm)
  - Method: it images the windowed variance of RSS, which human motion causes, rather than mean attenuation. A Kalman filter tracks the target from the motion image. Validated with a "34-node through-wall imaging and tracking system" over 780 sq ft. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5417)
  - Accuracy: no number appears in the abstract. Press coverage said "about three feet" ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2009-10-wi-fi-walls.amp)) or "within a meter or so" ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2009/10/01/29714/wireless-network-modded-to-see-through-walls/)). These are secondary sources; the paper's own figure was not verified.
  - Relevance to this project: variance-based RTI is the direct RSS analogue of the group's "rolling std" motion score. It detects motion only; a still person produces little variance.
- **Wilson & Patwari, "A Fade-Level Skew-Laplace Signal Strength Model for Device-Free Localization with Wireless Networks," IEEE Trans. Mobile Computing 11(6):947-958, 2012.** Shows that RSS changes caused by a person follow a skew-Laplace distribution whose parameters depend on the person's position and the link's fade level. Used in a particle filter that localizes both moving and stationary people. NOTE: the authors are Wilson and Patwari, not Kaltiokallio/Bocca as the brief assumed. — [dblp](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tmc/WilsonP12.html); [typeset copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-fade-level-skew-laplace-signal-strength-model-for-device-4oq8a9loi4.pdf)
- **Kaltiokallio, Bocca, Patwari, "Enhancing the Accuracy of Radio Tomographic Imaging Using Channel Diversity," IEEE MASS 2012.** Uses multiple 802.15.4 channels. Selecting channels by fade level gave better localization accuracy than selecting by packet reception rate: "a link's fade level is more important than a link's communication performance in channel selection." — [typeset copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/enhancing-the-accuracy-of-radio-tomographic-imaging-using-4wqgx4619v.pdf)
- **Zhao, Patwari, Phillips, Venkatasubramanian, "Radio Tomographic Imaging and Tracking of Stationary and Moving People via Kernel Distance," ACM/IEEE IPSN 2013, pp. 229-240, DOI 10.1145/2461381.2461410.** Uses a kernel distance between the current RSS histogram and a calibration histogram. This allows calibration-free localization of stationary and moving people, and it beat NRES-based systems in five NLOS experiments. An earlier IPSN 2012 demo abstract is "Histogram distance-based radio tomographic localization" (pp. 129-130). — [PDF (Utah)](https://www-old.cs.utah.edu/~jeffp/papers/RTI+KD-ipsn13.pdf); [DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2461381.2461410)
  - Relevance: a histogram or kernel distance against an "empty" baseline is a candidate stillness detector, separating a still person from an empty room. Variance-based detectors cannot do this.
- **Kaltiokallio, Bocca, Patwari, "Follow @grandma: Long-Term Device-Free Localization for Residential Monitoring."** The venue is the 7th IEEE Int'l Workshop on Practical Issues in Building Sensor Network Applications (SenseApp, 2012), not IPSN. The authors call it the first long-term RSS-DFL of a person during daily routines in a real home, and the system tweets area-of-interest (room/zone) transitions. — [typeset copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/follow-grandma-long-term-device-free-localization-for-5ghkxhgm60.pdf); [ISC record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/591854.htm). Page numbers were not verified.
  - Relevance: this is the closest RSS precedent for zone-level ("area of interest") output rather than coordinates.
- **Bocca, Kaltiokallio, Patwari, Venkatasubramanian, "Multiple Target Tracking with RF Sensor Networks"** (arXiv 1302.4720). Fade level = a link's path gain on a channel minus its minimum path gain across channels. It uses all channels weighted by fade level instead of keeping only the most "anti-fade" channels. — [arXiv 1302.4720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.4720). The published venue (believed to be IEEE TMC 2014) was not verified in this session.
- **Patwari, Brewer, Tate, Kaltiokallio, Bocca, "A Fade Level-Based Spatial Model for Radio Tomographic Imaging," IEEE TMC 2014.** The spatial weight model depends on fade level and on the sign of the RSS change. — [ISC record](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/22403.htm). Full text was not read.
- Related Utah RTI variants found but not read: directional RTI (dRTI, [arXiv 1402.2744](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2744)) and rotating RF sensors ([arXiv 1312.5480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.5480)).

### Inferences
- Classic RTI testbeds use 20-34 nodes in a dense mesh: each node transmits in turn (token passing) and all others listen, giving hundreds of links. A 1-TX x 6-RX star has only 6 links (12 with a reciprocal design). That is far below the regime where tomographic inversion works well, so zone classification or link-wise "which links are disturbed" fusion is more realistic than RTI image reconstruction.
- The fade-level finding (link "quality for sensing" is not the same as communication quality) carries over directly to CSI. Per-subcarrier amplitude gives about 56 "channels" per link in one frame, a frequency-diversity analogue of multi-channel RTI that needs no channel hopping.
- The group's motion score corresponds to variance-based RTI on a per-link basis. The path from motion detection to presence while still is the step from Wilson 2011 to Zhao 2013 (kernel/histogram distance against an empty baseline).

### Gaps
- Exact sampling rates and the median localization error in meters for RTI 2010, VRTI 2011 and KRTI 2013 were not verified from full text in this session. Pull them from the published PDFs before citing numbers.
- The published TMC 2010 version may differ from the 2008 preprint that was read.

## (b) CSI-based multi-link / multi-receiver device-free localization, zone and occupancy detection

### Takeaway
CSI-based DFL on commodity NICs (Intel 5300 / Atheros) dates from 2012-2013 (FIMD, Pilot, MonoPHY) and moved toward model-based localization (LiFS 2016). ESP32-specific multi-link work is recent and thin. Two January 2026 Preprints.org manuscripts (not peer-reviewed) are the closest prior work: a 3-link ESP32 grid localization study and a multi-zone ESP32 occupancy study with one AP-STA link per zone. Neither appears to vary receiver count systematically or compare RSSI and CSI on identical hardware.

### Cited Findings
- **Xiao, Wu, Yi, Wang, Ni, "FIMD: Fine-grained Device-free Motion Detection," IEEE ICPADS 2012, pp. 229-235, DOI 10.1109/ICPADS.2012.40.** Key premise: CSI is temporally stable in a static environment and shows burst patterns under motion. The feature exploits temporal stability plus frequency diversity, followed by a windowing filter to reduce false alarms. The authors claim better accuracy and robustness to narrowband interference than RSSI. — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6413692); [author PDF](https://luwang-szu.github.io/paper/FIMD_Fine-grained_Device-free_Motion_Detection.pdf); [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/icpads/XiaoWYWN12.html)
  - Relevance: FIMD is the most direct precedent for the group's empty / still / moving motion score.
- **Xiao, Wu, Yi, Wang, Ni, "Pilot: Passive Device-Free Indoor Localization Using Channel State Information," IEEE ICDCS 2013, pp. 236-245, DOI 10.1109/ICDCS.2013.49.** Two stages: anomaly (presence) detection, then location identification. Runs on commercial 802.11n NICs with multiple TX-RX pairs, and reports that it substantially outperforms the best RSS-based scheme. — [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/icdcs/XiaoWYWN13.html); [typeset](https://typeset.io/pdf/pilot-passive-device-free-indoor-localization-using-channel-1xufs996gh.pdf); multi-pair description per [Dang et al. 2019, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6679537/)
- **Abdel-Nasser, Samir, Sabek, Youssef, "MonoPHY: Mono-stream-based device-free WLAN localization via physical layer information," IEEE WCNC 2013, pp. 4546-4551.** Single AP plus one laptop, using CSI and MIMO information. Abstract reports 1.36 m median error, at least 48% better than prior single-stream DFL. — [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/wcnc/Abdel-NasserSSY13); [author PDF](https://rehammaburas.github.io/files/MonoPHY.pdf). Predecessor: Sabek & Youssef, "MonoStream" ([arXiv 1308.0768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.0768)), under 0.95 m in two testbeds.
  - Relevance: this is the single-link lower bound. It is useful as a "1 receiver" baseline in a receiver-count scaling curve.
- **Wang, Jiang, Xiong, Jamieson, Chen, Fang, Xie, "LiFS: Low Human-effort, Device-free Localization with Fine-grained Subcarrier Information," ACM MobiCom 2016, pp. 243-256, DOI 10.1145/2973750.2973776.** Extended in IEEE TMC 17(11):2550-2563, 2018. Identifies subcarriers barely affected by multipath and localizes from those, with low calibration effort. Reported median error is 0.5 m LOS and 1.1 m NLOS, without knowing most transceiver locations; this figure comes via the [Paper Digest](https://www.paperdigest.org/certificate/3b3f7d03ac104529) aggregator. — [UCL open manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1527423/1/LiFS%20Final%20Submission%20Manuscript.pdf)
  - Relevance: per-subcarrier selection is the CSI analogue of RTI fade-level channel selection, and it applies to amplitude-only ESP32 CSI.
- **"Device-Free Indoor Localization with ESP32 Wi-Fi CSI Fingerprints: Grid-Based Regression, Feature Modeling, and Multi-Link Experiments," Preprints.org 202601.2378 (Jan 2026, NOT peer-reviewed).** Three ESP32 transceivers around a 6 m x 4 m lab, CSI at 100 Hz, 35 grid points, two occupants, over 1.2 million snapshots, sub-meter median error with three links. It claims RSSI-based ESP32 localization "typically suffers from meter-level errors even with trilateration," but cites this from the literature rather than testing it on the same setup. — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2378); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400251069_Device-Free_Indoor_Localization_with_ESP32_Wi-Fi_CSI_Fingerprints_Grid-Based_Regression_Feature_Modeling_and_Multi-Link_Experiments). The full text returned HTTP 403, so the authors, the exact topology (mesh vs star) and any link-ablation results are UNVERIFIED.
- **Chaudhari, Pise, Fukate, Gawande, "Room-Level Occupancy Estimation via Wi-Fi Channel State Information on ESP32 Nodes: A Multi-Zone Experimental Study with Formal CSI-Occupancy Modeling," Preprints.org 202601.2295, posted 29 Jan 2026 (NOT peer-reviewed).** ESP32-WROOM-32 AP/STA pairs in 4 zones (3 residential rooms plus 1 office), 2.4 GHz / 20 MHz, about 250 h of data labeled by door sensors and schedules, classes 0 / 1 / 2+. Gradient boosting reaches zone-averaged F1 0.96 (binary) and 0.89 (3-class), with 38 ms median latency on a Raspberry Pi. Also covers cross-zone transfer and calibration. A search summary reports that the paper lists reliance on single-link CSI per zone as a limitation. — [Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2295); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400250338_Room-Level_Occupancy_Estimation_via_Wi-Fi_Channel_State_Information_on_ESP32_Nodes_A_Multi-Zone_Experimental_Study_with_Formal_CSI-Occupancy_Modeling). The limitation statement was not confirmed from full text.
- **Wi-ESP: a tool for CSI-based device-free Wi-Fi sensing on ESP32**, J. Computational Design and Engineering 7(5):644, 2020. Real-time ESP32 CSI capture without external hardware. — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jcde/article/7/5/644/5837600)
- **"Why Commodity WiFi Sensors Fail at Multi-Person Gait Identification: A Systematic Analysis Using ESP32"** (arXiv 2601.02177). As summarized in search results, it uses one ESP32-S3 TX and three RX, sends CSI over USB to a PC, and aligns frames by a packet sequence number. — [arXiv 2601.02177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02177). Details are from the search summary; the full text was not read.
- **ESPARGOS** ("An Ultra Low-Cost, Realtime-Capable Multi-Antenna WiFi Channel Sounder," arXiv 2502.09405). An array of ESP32s that receive frames independently. Frames must be clustered after reception. Phase is corrupted by the unknown transmitter oscillator offset and drift, so the system distributes a shared reference clock. Receivers may miss frames. — [arXiv 2502.09405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09405)
- **Espressif ESP-CSI** official solution. Its dual-receiver mode connects the two chips through a clock buffer to remove the relative frequency offset, implying phase is unusable across unsynced chips. — [Espressif ESP-Techpedia](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/solution-introduction/esp-csi/esp-csi-solution.html)
- **CoMuTe** (CNN-based multi-target device-free localization with CSI). States that multi-target localization is a major open challenge. — [arXiv 2003.05734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05734)
- Hobbyist precedent: a GitHub project with 2 ESP32-S3 receivers at about 100 pkt/s pinging a router; no labeled dataset or accuracy reported. — [GitHub Lakshyaa1/wifi-csi-localization-sensing](https://github.com/Lakshyaa1/wifi-csi-localization-sensing)
- Related but not read: University of Twente thesis on room occupancy from Wi-Fi CSI ([UT essay 101206](https://essay.utwente.nl/essays/101206)).

### Inferences
- In the classic CSI-DFL literature (Pilot, LiFS), "multi-link" means several AP-client pairs on Intel 5300/Atheros NICs, usually with phase available. Amplitude-only, single-antenna, low-cost CSI on a star topology is a different and less-studied regime.
- The two 2026 ESP32 preprints show the topic is active, so any novelty claim must be narrow and explicitly positioned against them. Because they are unreviewed and full text was not accessible here, the team should download them and confirm the topology and ablations.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED:** "Wi-Dist", named in the brief, could not be identified; no search was run specifically for it. Do not cite it without finding the paper.
- Daqing Zhang's Fresnel-zone sensing papers, Widar, and Zheng Yang / Kun Qian localization papers were not searched in this session (budget). A Tsinghua tutorial, "From RSSI to CSI: Indoor Localization via Channel Response" (Yang, Zhou, Liu, ACM Computing Surveys 2013), surfaced ([PDF](https://tns.thss.tsinghua.edu.cn/widar3.0/data/RSSI2CSI.pdf)), but its bibliographic details were not checked.

## (c) Scaling with number of links/nodes, placement, link selection, and star vs mesh topology

### Takeaway
RTI papers argue that more nodes are better (link count grows as N(N-1)/2) and propose link or channel selection to cut cost and multipath noise. No study found systematically measures accuracy against the number of receivers for a one-broadcaster, many-cheap-receivers star, either for RSS or for CSI.

### Cited Findings
- RTI claims imaging power grows "as Metcalf's Law with the number of nodes" and suggests many low-complexity devices. Every pair of the K nodes is a link. — [Wilson & Patwari RTI PDF](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)
- Channel or link selection by fade level beats selection by packet reception rate. — [Kaltiokallio, Bocca, Patwari MASS 2012](https://typeset.io/pdf/enhancing-the-accuracy-of-radio-tomographic-imaging-using-4wqgx4619v.pdf)
- Link-selection papers: heuristic link selection for RF tomography based on Bayesian compressive sensing (Advanced Materials Research 1049-1050, pp. 520 and 526). They claim better accuracy for the same number of measurements and note that scanning many links burdens energy, routing and storage. — [Principle and Method](https://www.scientific.net/AMR.1049-1050.520); [Application](https://www.scientific.net/AMR.1049-1050.526); a related Chinese Journal of Electronics 2013 paper ([ejournal.org.cn](https://www.ejournal.org.cn/EN/10.3969/j.issn.0372-2112.2013.12.030))
- A 2018 paper, "Link Selection in Radio Tomographic Imaging with Backprojection Transformation," argues that selecting informative links resists multipath noise. — [CAS catalog record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........c36d1425cbbf65d066aed32a6e82c069). The authors and venue were not verified.
- A low-node-count RTI study (8 nodes) notes that prior work used more than 20 nodes and that processing time grows with sensor count, with link count Z = N(N-1)/2. — [ECTI-EEC paper (Thai journal)](https://ph02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/ECTI-EEC/article/download/249427/170779/941282). The title and authors were not extracted, so the citation is UNVERIFIED beyond the URL.
- ESP-NOW natively supports one-to-many sending. The commonly cited limit of about 20 peers applies to unicast peer tables; broadcast to FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF reaches any number of promiscuous listeners. — [microcontrollerslab one-to-many](https://microcontrollerslab.com/esp-now-one-to-many-esp32-esp8266/); [Wolles Elektronikkiste](https://wolles-elektronikkiste.de/en/esp-now). The claim that broadcast is unaffected by the peer limit is an inference from ESP-NOW broadcast semantics, not verified here.

### Inferences
- In a star topology, links grow linearly (N receivers give N links) instead of quadratically. Each added receiver adds exactly one new link geometry, and adding one costs only configuration because the transmitter is unchanged. This makes clean "accuracy vs N" curves cheap to produce. Mesh RTI papers cannot isolate this because adding a node adds N-1 links at once.
- A practical design for 7 boards: 1 TX plus 6 RX gives 6 TX-RX links. Optionally rotate which board transmits across sessions to emulate a mesh, which allows a star-vs-pseudo-mesh comparison with the same hardware.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports zone accuracy against receiver count for CSI on low-cost hardware. Searches run: "radio tomographic imaging link selection number of nodes effect accuracy subset of links"; "RSS versus CSI device-free localization same hardware comparison ESP32 RSSI CSI ... number of links"; "ESP32 CSI multiple receivers device-free localization zone occupancy". Coverage is limited to these English web searches; IEEE Xplore and ACM DL were not searched directly.

## (d) Fusion across unsynchronized receivers and required timing accuracy

### Takeaway
Existing low-cost multi-receiver ESP32 systems align frames by a transmitter-embedded sequence number rather than by clocks, treat missing frames as normal, and give up on cross-receiver phase unless a shared clock is distributed (ESPARGOS, ESP-CSI dual-RX). For amplitude-only motion and zone features computed over windows of hundreds of milliseconds, millisecond arrival-time alignment is very likely sufficient. No paper was found that quantifies the needed tolerance.

### Cited Findings
- Multi-receiver ESP32 systems match packets across receivers by a sequence number in the payload, and a PC gathers each packet's measurements from all receivers. — [arXiv 2601.02177 (search summary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02177)
- ESPARGOS receivers obtain frames independently, so frames must be clustered after reception. The transmitter's initial phase is unknown because of oscillator offset and drift. Receivers can miss frames. — [ESPARGOS, arXiv 2502.09405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09405)
- ESP-CSI dual-RX ties two chips to one clock buffer to remove the relative frequency offset. Without a shared clock, cross-chip phase is not meaningful. — [Espressif ESP-CSI docs](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/solution-introduction/esp-csi/esp-csi-solution.html)

### Inferences
- The project's TX broadcasts from a fixed MAC, and the 802.11 header carries a sequence-control field. Putting a 32-bit counter in the ESP-NOW payload as well would give exact frame-level alignment across receivers with no clock sync. That is better than MQTT hub arrival time, which adds Wi-Fi and broker jitter.
- Human motion changes the channel on roughly 10-100 ms timescales (body speeds around 1 m/s against a 12.5 cm wavelength at 2.4 GHz give Doppler of tens of Hz; standard physics, not from a cited source). Window-level features such as 0.5-2 s rolling std therefore tolerate tens of milliseconds of misalignment. Frame-level cross-receiver features (inter-receiver correlation) would need sequence-number alignment.
- One experiment could measure this directly: inject artificial offsets (0, 10, 50, 100, 500 ms) between receivers' streams and plot zone accuracy. This would quantify the timing tolerance; no prior work quantifying it was found.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that quantifies the timing requirement for fusing amplitude-CSI features across unsynchronized receivers. Searches: "one transmitter multiple ESP32 receivers CSI broadcast ESP-NOW sensing fusion unsynchronized".

## (e) Candidate under-explored questions for a ~5-week, 7-board project, with novelty evidence

### Takeaway
The best-supported gaps are: (1) an accuracy-vs-receiver-count scaling curve plus leave-one-receiver-out robustness for a 1-broadcaster star of identical low-cost receivers; (2) a same-frame, same-hardware RSSI vs amplitude-CSI comparison, since every ESP32 CSI callback also reports RSSI; (3) stillness (empty vs still person) detection with multiple links, using RTI-style histogram/kernel distances adapted to CSI. Each is narrow but defensible. Novelty claims must name the closest prior work below and acknowledge the limited search coverage.

### Cited Findings
Each candidate gives: what prior work covers, the closest work, the searches run, and a verdict.

1. **Accuracy vs number of receivers (N = 1...6) in a 1-TX star, for presence and 3-zone classification.**
   - Closest work: the 3-link ESP32 fingerprinting preprint ([Preprints 202601.2378](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2378)); the multi-zone ESP32 occupancy preprint with one link per zone ([Preprints 202601.2295](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2295)); RTI's argument for more nodes ([RTI PDF](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)); MonoPHY single-link baseline ([dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/wcnc/Abdel-NasserSSY13)).
   - Searches: "ESP32 CSI multiple receivers device-free localization zone occupancy"; "radio tomographic imaging link selection number of nodes ..."; "one transmitter multiple ESP32 receivers CSI broadcast ESP-NOW ...".
   - Verdict: no scaling curve found. Likely novel as a systematic, configuration-only ablation. Medium confidence, because the full texts of the two preprints were not accessible.
2. **Leave-one-receiver-out (LORO) robustness and minimum receiver count for a target zone accuracy, including which placements matter most (greedy receiver selection).**
   - Closest work: RTI link and channel selection ([MASS 2012](https://typeset.io/pdf/enhancing-the-accuracy-of-radio-tomographic-imaging-using-4wqgx4619v.pdf); [BCS link selection](https://www.scientific.net/AMR.1049-1050.520)). Those select links in a dense mesh for imaging, not receivers in a star for zone classification.
   - Verdict: not found for CSI/ESP32. Plausibly novel. Cheap to run, since it is offline subsampling of one dataset.
3. **RSSI vs amplitude-CSI from the same frames on the same ESP32 receivers, under the same multi-link fusion.**
   - Closest work: FIMD claims CSI beats RSSI on Intel-NIC hardware ([FIMD](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6413692)); Pilot claims CSI beats the best RSS scheme ([dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/icdcs/XiaoWYWN13.html)); the ESP32 preprint contrasts with RSSI only via literature ([Preprints 202601.2378](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2378)).
   - Search: "RSS versus CSI device-free localization same hardware comparison ESP32 RSSI CSI motion detection number of links". It returned no controlled same-hardware ESP32 comparison.
   - Verdict: the ESP32 multi-link version appears unaddressed. This is a strong candidate because it comes almost for free: wifi_csi_info_t includes rx_ctrl.rssi per frame (standard ESP-IDF API; documentation not fetched in this session). It also connects RTI (RSS) and CSI work directly.
4. **Stillness: empty vs still-person vs moving with multiple links.**
   - Closest work: Zhao et al. kernel-distance RTI localizes stationary people with RSS ([IPSN 2013](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2461381.2461410)); the skew-Laplace model handles stationary people ([TMC 2012](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tmc/WilsonP12.html)); Pilot does presence anomaly detection ([dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/icdcs/XiaoWYWN13.html)).
   - Verdict: the method is known in RSS-RTI. Applying histogram or kernel distance to per-subcarrier ESP32 amplitude across several links, and showing how many links stillness detection needs, appears unexplored. Moderately novel. Given the group's 3-4x motion/still ratio from variance alone, still-vs-empty is where a variance score is expected to fail.
5. **Effect of co-channel home Wi-Fi traffic (MQTT uplink on the same 2.4 GHz channel) on CSI frame rate, loss and sensing accuracy.**
   - Closest work: FIMD claims robustness to narrowband interference ([FIMD](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6413692)). Nothing ESP32-specific was found.
   - Searches: none run specifically (budget). Verdict: UNVERIFIED novelty; needs a dedicated search before claiming it.
6. **Cross-session and cross-day drift of ESP32 amplitude-CSI zone classifiers.**
   - Closest work: Follow @grandma long-term RSS-DFL ([typeset](https://typeset.io/pdf/follow-grandma-long-term-device-free-localization-for-5ghkxhgm60.pdf)); the ESP32 occupancy preprint covers cross-zone transfer and calibration ([Preprints 202601.2295](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2295)).
   - Verdict: partially covered. A 1-2 week drift study with LORO is feasible but probably incremental.
7. **Timing tolerance for multi-receiver fusion (artificial offset injection).**
   - Closest work: sequence-number alignment ([arXiv 2601.02177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02177)); frame clustering in ESPARGOS ([arXiv 2502.09405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09405)).
   - Verdict: no quantification found. Small, but a clean methodological contribution.
8. **Star (1 broadcaster) vs rotating-transmitter pseudo-mesh on the same 7 boards.**
   - Closest work: all RTI meshes ([RTI](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf), [VRTI](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5417)). No same-hardware star-vs-mesh comparison was found.
   - Verdict: plausibly novel, but it needs firmware changes so the transmitter role can be swapped. A stretch goal.

### Inferences
- Recommended core for 5 weeks: collect one labeled dataset of about 3 sessions x (empty / still in zone A, B, C / walking in A, B, C) with 6 receivers. Log both RSSI and CSI per frame, with a payload sequence counter. Offline, compute (i) zone and presence accuracy against N with all subsets (LORO), (ii) RSSI-only vs CSI-only vs both, and (iii) a variance score vs a kernel/histogram-distance stillness detector. A single dataset answers candidates 1-4 and 7, and receivers are added by configuration only.
- Frame the contribution as "a configuration-only scaling and modality ablation for a broadcast-star ESP32-C6 CSI network," not as a new localization algorithm. Explicitly cite the two 2026 ESP32 preprints as the closest prior work.
- Report results as zone accuracy or F1 with confidence intervals across sessions. A single recording, such as the preliminary 3-4x ratio, is not evidence of generalization.

### Gaps
- Searches did not cover IEEE Xplore, ACM DL or Google Scholar directly. Zheng Yang, Kun Qian and Daqing Zhang publication lists were not searched. ESP32-C6-specific CSI papers (the C6 supports Wi-Fi 6; its CSI behavior may differ from the classic ESP32) were not searched. Before claiming novelty in the report, run at least: "ESP32 CSI number of receivers", "CSI receiver placement occupancy", "RSSI CSI comparison ESP32", "ESP32-C6 CSI", and "leave-one-out receiver Wi-Fi sensing" on Google Scholar and IEEE Xplore.
- The full texts of Preprints 202601.2378 and 202601.2295 were not accessed (HTTP 403). Their topology (star vs mesh) and any per-link ablations must be checked. If either already reports accuracy vs number of links, candidates 1-2 lose much of their novelty.
