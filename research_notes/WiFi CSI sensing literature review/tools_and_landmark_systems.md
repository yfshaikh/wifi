# Wi-Fi CSI Measurement Tools and Landmark Wi-Fi Sensing Systems

Scope note: these notes are for the RIDE Fall 2026 Group 6 project, which uses ESP32-C6 boards (single antenna, 2.4 GHz, 20 MHz 802.11n, amplitude-only CSI). Verification levels used below:
- **VERIFIED (primary)**: checked against the original paper's PDF, abstract, or the tool's own README or project page.
- **VERIFIED (metadata)**: title, authors, venue and year confirmed from a bibliographic or publisher record, but the numbers come from the abstract only.
- **SECONDARY**: the number comes from a survey, press article or summary site, not the original paper.
- **UNVERIFIED**: comes from my prior knowledge and could not be confirmed in this session. Check it before citing.

## Q1. CSI extraction tools: hardware, antennas, subcarriers, bandwidth, sampling rate, cost, OS constraints, citation

### Takeaway
Before about 2019, almost all CSI research used two laptop-hosted 802.11n NIC tools: the Intel 5300 tool (3 antennas, 30 subcarrier groups) and the Atheros tool (3 antennas, 56/114 subcarriers). Both need a Linux PC with a modified kernel driver. Nexmon (Broadcom phones, Raspberry Pi and routers, up to 80 MHz) and PicoScenes (Intel AX200/AX210, QCA9300, SDR, up to 160 MHz) extended CSI to newer hardware. The ESP32 CSI Tool and Espressif's esp-csi are the only standalone, battery-powered options that cost under $10. They trade antenna count and resolution (1 antenna, 8-bit I/Q) for price and portability, which fits the ESP32-C6 hardware this project uses.

### Cited Findings

**Linux 802.11n CSI Tool (Intel 5300)**: VERIFIED (primary)
- Runs on the Intel WiFi Wireless Link 5300 802.11n MIMO NIC with modified firmware and the open-source Linux wireless driver. It reports channel matrices for **30 subcarrier groups**: about one group per 2 subcarriers at 20 MHz, or one per 4 at 40 MHz — [Linux 802.11n CSI Tool project page](https://dhalperi.github.io/linux-80211n-csitool/)
- Citation: D. Halperin, W. Hu, A. Sheth, D. Wetherall, "Tool Release: Gathering 802.11n Traces with Channel State Information," *ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review*, 41(1), Jan 2011. DOI 10.1145/1925861.1925870 — [project page](https://dhalperi.github.io/linux-80211n-csitool/). The volume/issue and DOI are UNVERIFIED (from memory); the title, authors, venue and date are verified.
- Hernandez & Bulut's comparison table gives the Intel 5300 (+laptop) as: 30 subcarrier groups, 8-bit resolution (imaginary/real), up to 1000 frames/s, 3 antennas, about $10 plus a laptop, kernel-level implementation, not standalone, >1 kg with the laptop. Of 494 papers listed on the tools' websites, **92.5% used the Intel 5300**, 6.8% Atheros, and <1% Nexmon — [Hernandez & Bulut, WoWMoM 2020, Table I](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)
- The OS constraint (old Linux kernels, Ubuntu 14.04-era, custom kernel build) comes from the tool's install page, which I did not fetch: UNVERIFIED.

**Atheros CSI Tool (QCA ath9k chips)**: VERIFIED (metadata)
- An open-source 802.11n measurement tool built on **ath9k**, the Linux kernel driver for Atheros 802.11n PCI/PCI-E chips. Maintained by Yaxiong Xie — [Atheros CSI Tool page, wands.sg](https://wands.sg/research/wifi/AtherosCSI)
- Citation the tool asks for: Y. Xie, Z. Li, M. Li, "Precise Power Delay Profiling with Commodity WiFi," *MobiCom '15*, pp. 53–64, DOI 10.1145/2789168.2790124. The paper's system, Splicer, splices CSI from multiple bands to get a high-resolution power delay profile — [Cite page](https://wands.sg/research/wifi/AtherosCSI/CiteAtherosCSItool.html); [paper PDF](https://www3.ntu.edu.sg/home/limo/papers/com292-xieA.pdf). Extended version in IEEE TMC (Xplore date 30 Jul 2018) — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8423070)
- Comparison table: **56 subcarrier groups** (at 20 MHz), 11-bit resolution, up to 1000 frames/s, 3 antennas, kernel-level, $10 plus a laptop — [Hernandez & Bulut 2020, Table I](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf). The 114 subcarriers at 40 MHz figure is not confirmed from a primary source here (UNVERIFIED).

**Nexmon CSI (Broadcom/Cypress)**: VERIFIED (primary)
- Supported chips and devices: bcm4339 (Nexus 5), bcm43455c0 (Raspberry Pi 3B+/4B/5), bcm4358 (Nexus 6P), bcm4366c0 (Asus RT-AC86U). Works at **20/40/80 MHz**. Reports **64/128/256 subcarriers** respectively (including guard and null carriers), 4 bytes per value. Core and spatial-stream selection is set by `makecsiparams -C/-N`. CSI is extracted per received frame, so the rate depends on the transmitter's traffic — [nexmon_csi GitHub README](https://github.com/seemoo-lab/nexmon_csi)
- Citation: F. Gringoli, M. Schulz, J. Link, M. Hollick, "Free Your CSI: A Channel State Information Extraction Platform for Modern Wi-Fi Chipsets," *WiNTECH '19* (13th Workshop on Wireless Network Testbeds, Experimental evaluation & CHaracterization), Oct 2019, DOI 10.1145/3349623.3355477. Also cite M. Schulz, D. Wegemer, M. Hollick, *Nexmon: The C-based Firmware Patching Framework*, nexmon.org, 2017 — [nexmon_csi README](https://github.com/seemoo-lab/nexmon_csi)
- Comparison table: 128 subcarrier groups, 32-bit resolution, firmware-level, >$100, "1 antenna" (as tested on a Nexus), up to 1000 frames/s. Firmware patching "may damage your hardware and may void your hardware's warranty" — [Hernandez & Bulut 2020](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)

**PicoScenes**: VERIFIED (primary)
- Supported hardware: COTS NICs Intel AX210, AX200, QCA9300 and IWL5300, plus SDRs (HackRF One and all USRP models). AX200/AX210 give CSI up to **160 MHz**; QCA9300 covers 2.5–80 MHz; SDR front-ends 20–320 MHz; AX210 also covers the 6 GHz band (5945–7125 MHz). Packet injection reaches up to 4 kHz in real time; CSI measurement reaches up to **1 kHz at 20 MHz** single-thread. Officially supported on **Ubuntu 22.04** (moving to 24.04), distributed as .deb packages. A December 2023 licence update removed the limits on COTS NICs. No prices are listed — [PicoScenes site](https://ps.zpj.io/)
- Citation: Z. Jiang, T. H. Luan, X. Ren, D. Lv, H. Hao, J. Wang, K. Zhao, W. Xi, Y. Xu, R. Li, "Eliminating the Barriers: Demystifying Wi-Fi Baseband Design and Introducing the PicoScenes Wi-Fi Sensing Platform," *IEEE Internet of Things Journal*, 9(6):4476–4496, 2022, DOI 10.1109/JIOT.2021.3104666; arXiv 2010.10233 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10233v4); [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/JiangLRLHWZXXL22). The full author list beyond Jiang, Luan and Zhao is UNVERIFIED (from memory); check it on the arXiv page.

**ESP32 CSI Tool (Hernandez & Bulut)**: VERIFIED (primary)
- Runs on the ESP32 microcontroller using ESP-IDF, with no firmware hacks. It can collect CSI as an AP or as a STA, write to serial or a microSD card, run standalone on a battery, or attach to an Android phone over USB. The codebase is about 1,000 lines — [Hernandez & Bulut, WoWMoM 2020, pp. 277–286](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)
- Their Table I values for the ESP32: **64 subcarriers**, **8-bit** imaginary/real, **up to 650 frames/s**, **1 antenna** (2–16 with a switch), cost **<$10**, 5.0 × 3.0 cm, <10 g, 500 KB–4 MB RAM, user-level code. The text says a stable rate above 500 Hz is possible when both ends are controlled. Earlier Nexmon sniffing studies managed only 8–11 packets/s — [Hernandez & Bulut 2020](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)
- Citation: S. M. Hernandez, E. Bulut, "Lightweight and Standalone IoT based WiFi Sensing for Active Repositioning and Mobility," *2020 IEEE 21st Int. Symp. on "A World of Wireless, Mobile and Multimedia Networks" (WoWMoM)*, Cork, Ireland, pp. 277–286, DOI 10.1109/WoWMoM49955.2020.00056. Code: [GitHub](https://github.com/StevenMHernandez/ESP32-CSI-Tool); [project page](https://stevenmhernandez.github.io/ESP32-CSI-Tool)
- Result in the paper: allowing the receiver to be repositioned or to move gave accuracy gains of "upwards of 29.4% and 28.2%" over a static receiver in DNN-based HAR experiments (abstract) — [paper](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)
- Follow-up: Hernandez & Bulut, "WiFi Sensing on the Edge: Signal Processing Techniques and Challenges for Real-World Systems," *IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials*. It evaluates on-board ESP32 processing time and throughput — [search result](https://wisecomlab.github.io/files/esp32.pdf). Year/volume: UNVERIFIED (I believe 2023).

**Espressif esp-csi (official)**: VERIFIED (primary, partial)
- Espressif says all ESP32 series support CSI: ESP32, ESP32-S2, ESP32-C3, ESP32-S3, ESP32-C5, **ESP32-C6**, ESP32-C61. The repo targets "indoor positioning, human detection". It includes the `esp-radar` examples (human activity detection, RainMaker reporting) and a `wifi_sensing_demo` (motion and presence detection with on-site training) built on the `esp_wifi_sensing` component. Community project: ESPectre (CSI motion detection for Home Assistant) — [espressif/esp-csi GitHub](https://github.com/espressif/esp-csi)
- On ESP32-C5-class chips (newer Wi-Fi MAC), each non-legacy PPDU carries an L-LTF plus an HT/VHT/HE-LTF, and the chip chooses between them with `acquire_csi_force_lltf` in `wifi_csi_acquire_config_t` — [ESP-IDF Wi-Fi Vendor Features, ESP32-C5](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v6.0/esp32c5/api-guides/wifi-driver/wifi-vendor-features.html)
- ESP32-C6 specifics, SECONDARY only. A third-party Rust crate says that without HE-LTF selection the C6 reports the legacy L-LTF (**53 subcarriers / 106 bytes**) for HE20 frames instead of the full HE-LTF (about 242 subcarriers) — [esp-csi-rs docs](https://docs.rs/crate/esp-csi-rs/0.13.0/source/src/lib/config/csi.rs). A community release note says the C6 reaches 242 HE20 tones on ESP-IDF v5.5.2 — [RuView release](https://github.com/ruvnet/RuView/releases/tag/v0.8.0-esp32). I could not confirm either against official Espressif docs. The ESP-IDF C6 CSI doc URLs returned 404.
- Data format: CSI is stored as complex values (imaginary and real). Amplitude is √(im²+re²) and phase is atan2(im, re) — [Hernandez & Bulut 2020, Eqs. 2–3](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf). The signed int8 imaginary-then-real ordering per subcarrier is ESP-IDF documented behaviour that I did not re-verify here.

### Inferences
- The ESP32 lineage gives one RX antenna and 8-bit I/Q CSI per packet. Phase is reported but corrupted by CFO/SFO/STO, with no second antenna to cancel these offsets, so amplitude is the dependable feature. This matches the project's amplitude-only design choice.
- In packets per second, ESP32 tools (up to about 650 frames/s per Hernandez & Bulut) are comparable to NIC tools (about 1000/s). The real gaps are antenna count, resolution, and the lack of reliable phase.
- The project's 20 MHz HT20 setup should expect about 52–64 usable subcarriers (LLTF/HT-LTF), not the 242 HE tones, unless HE-LTF capture is explicitly enabled on a recent ESP-IDF.

### Gaps
- Official Espressif documentation of ESP32-C6 CSI subcarrier/byte layout (LLTF / HT-LTF / HE-LTF) was not reachable (404). Check the ESP-IDF `wifi_csi_acquire_config_t` header in the project's IDF version.
- The Intel 5300 tool's kernel/OS requirements and the Atheros 114-subcarrier figure at 40 MHz were not confirmed from primary pages.
- PicoScenes pricing: the site lists no prices.

## Q2. Landmark sensing systems (presence, motion, activity/gesture, localization, vital signs)

### Takeaway
Landmark systems fall into a few groups:
- **Model-based**: CARM, Fresnel-zone work, FarSense, IndoTrack, LiFS, SpotFi, Chronos, WiDir.
- **Learning or fingerprint-based**: E-eyes, WiFall, WiKey, WiDeep, FreeSense, Widar 3.0's DNN on a physical feature.

Nearly all CSI systems before 2020 used the 3-antenna Intel 5300. Wi-Vi and WiTrack used custom radios, not CSI. WiGest used RSSI only. WiSee used a USRP SDR. Reported accuracies (often >90%) come from 1–2 rooms with few subjects and drop noticeably in new environments.

**Presence and motion detection systems**
- **FIMD**: J. Xiao, K. Wu, Y. Yi, L. Wang, L. M. Ni, "FIMD: Fine-grained Device-free Motion Detection," *IEEE ICPADS 2012*, pp. 229–235. Accuracy figures not retrieved — [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6413692), [PDF](https://luwang-szu.github.io/paper/FIMD_Fine-grained_Device-free_Motion_Detection.pdf), [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/icpads/XiaoWYWN12.html)
- **PADS**: K. Qian, C. Wu, Z. Yang, Y. Liu, Z. Zhou, "PADS: Passive Detection of Moving Targets with Dynamic Speed using PHY Layer Information," *IEEE ICPADS 2014*, DOI 10.1109/PADSW.2014.7097784. - **R-TTWD**: H. Zhu, F. Xiao, L. Sun, R. Wang, P. ## Q3. Theory: Fresnel zone model and the CARM CSI-speed model; and which systems need multi-antenna/phase vs. work with single-antenna amplitude CSI

### Takeaway
There are two main theory frameworks. The first is the **CSI-speed model** (CARM, MobiCom 2015): CSI power is a sum of path components, and each dynamic path's frequency is proportional to the speed of the body part changing that path's length. The second is the **Fresnel zone model** from Daqing Zhang's group (UbiComp 2016 onward): received signal strength rises and falls as a reflector crosses successive Fresnel ellipses around the TX–RX pair, which predicts where and in which orientation breathing or motion can be detected. Amplitude/power-only methods (CARM, E-eyes, WiFall, FIMD, Fresnel-amplitude respiration, WiKey) are compatible with an ESP32-C6 in principle. AoA/ToF, phase-difference and CSI-ratio methods (SpotFi, Chronos, IndoTrack, Widar 3.0, RT-Fall, FarSense, PADS) need two or more coherent antennas or calibrated phase.

### Cited Findings
- **CSI-speed model (CARM)**: quantifies the correlation between CSI value dynamics and human movement speeds. The **CSI-activity model** quantifies the correlation between the speeds of different body parts and a specific activity. CARM uses **CSI power** because "CSI signal power is not affected by CFO" while it keeps the movement-speed information. Because typical human movement maps to CSI components below 300 Hz, the model recommends **800 samples/s**. It uses PCA denoising (exploiting correlation across subcarriers), DWT features and HMM classification — [CARM PDF, pp. 65–66](https://old.sigmobile.org/mobicom/2015/papers/p65-wangA.pdf)
- **Fresnel zone model for respiration**: H. Wang, D. Zhang, J. Ma, Y. Wang, Y. Wang, D. Wu, T. Gu, B. Xie, "Human Respiration Detection with Commodity WiFi Devices: Do User Location and Body Orientation Matter?", *UbiComp 2016*, pp. 25–36, DOI 10.1145/2971648.2971744. The paper uses the Fresnel model to relate breathing depth, location and orientation to the received signal, and asks whether respiration can be sensed wherever the subject is and whichever way they face. The companion demo says detectable and undetectable regions are determined by the Fresnel-zone theory. The full author list beyond Wang and Zhang is UNVERIFIED — [IP Paris portal](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/human-respiration-detection-with-commodity-wifi-devices-do-user-l/), [DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2971648.2971744)
- **CSI ratio (FarSense)**: dividing the CSI of two antennas on the same NIC cancels the shared phase offsets and much of the amplitude noise, which extends the respiration range to about 8 m. **This needs 2 RX antennas** — [FarSense arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03994)

  - F. Zhang et al., "From Fresnel Diffraction Model to Fine-grained Human Respiration Sensing with Commodity Wi-Fi Devices," *IMWUT* 2(1), 2018.

### Inferences
- For an ESP32-C6 (one antenna, amplitude only), the closest templates are:
  - **FIMD-style presence/motion detection**: variance or correlation bursts across subcarriers.
  - **CARM-style HAR**: power spectrum and DWT features with a classifier. A practical ESP32 sampling rate (about 100–650 Hz per Hernandez & Bulut) is below CARM's 800 Hz recommendation, which limits fine speed resolution but is enough for coarse activities.
  - **Fresnel-amplitude respiration sensing** at short range, keeping its location- and orientation-dependent "blind spots" in mind.
- Systems such as FarSense, RT-Fall, IndoTrack, SpotFi and Widar 3.0 are not directly reproducible on one ESP32-C6 antenna. A multi-board setup with independent clocks does not give coherent phase across antennas.
