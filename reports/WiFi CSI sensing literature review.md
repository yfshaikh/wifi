# Cheap single-antenna CSI networks remain largely unmeasured

**Literature review — RIDE Fall 2026, Group 6, "Wi-Fi Sensing Using Channel State Information"**
Team: Yusuf Shaikh, Thomas Alcorn, Karl Wara, Vihaan Vuppala. Review compiled 5 November 2026 for a 7 December 2026 demo and an 11 December 2026 report.

Fifteen years of Wi-Fi channel state information (CSI) research have produced dependable motion detection, activity recognition above 90% in the rooms where systems were trained, and decimetre-level localization. Almost all of it ran on three-antenna Intel 5300 or Atheros laptop cards, and the strongest results depend on phase or on several coherent antennas. A single-antenna, amplitude-only ESP32-C6 has neither. The methods that carry over to Group 6's hardware are the amplitude- and power-based ones: FIMD-style motion bursts, CARM-style power spectra, E-eyes-style amplitude distributions, and RSS-style radio tomography with per-subcarrier diversity. ESP32 work is growing quickly (through-wall activity recognition at 92.0 ± 3.5% NLOS accuracy, public datasets, phase-coherent ESP32 arrays), but the specific configuration this project uses has not been characterized in the sources found. That configuration is one broadcaster heard by many cheap, unsynchronized receivers that are added by configuration alone. In particular, no source found measures how presence or zone accuracy changes as receivers are added or removed, or compares RSSI and CSI amplitude from the same frames on ESP32 hardware. A 2026 preprint on a 72-link ESP32-C3 mesh even reports that more links made sensing worse. The recommended original contribution is a **configuration-only receiver-scaling and modality ablation**: zone and presence accuracy as a function of receiver count (N = 1…6) and of RSSI versus CSI amplitude, measured on one labeled multi-session dataset, with a stillness detector borrowed from RSS radio tomography as the Module 2 extension. This is feasible in about five weeks because one dataset answers every question offline. The novelty claim carries medium confidence: the two closest prior works, both January 2026 Preprints.org manuscripts, could not be read in full and must be checked before the claim is made.

## 1. Introduction and scope: why survey before building

### What a literature review is for

A literature review does three jobs for an engineering project. It shows what is already known, so the team does not rebuild a published result and present it as new. It identifies which published methods are physically possible on the team's hardware. And it locates a gap: a question the literature has left open that the team's design is unusually well placed to answer. For Group 6 the third job matters most. The project's hardware (ESP32-C6, one antenna, 2.4 GHz, 20 MHz HT20, amplitude-only CSI) sits at the cheapest, most constrained end of the field. Many headline results do not apply to it, so any contribution has to come from what this hardware does well rather than from competing with multi-antenna systems.

### Research questions

The review answers four questions. **RQ1:** What tools, landmark systems and theory define the state of the art in Wi-Fi CSI sensing for presence, motion, activity recognition and localization? **RQ2:** What has been done specifically on ESP32-family hardware, and in multi-link device-free localization? **RQ3:** What open problems remain? **RQ4:** Where can a low-cost, multi-receiver ESP32-C6 system add an original contribution that is feasible by the 7 December 2026 demo?

### The system the review relates everything back to

One ESP32-C6 transmitter broadcasts 4-byte ESP-NOW frames at 100 Hz (802.11n MCS0, fixed source MAC 1a:00:00:00:00:01). The payload is already an incrementing counter, but receivers do not yet record it. Any number of ESP32-C6 receivers capture CSI in promiscuous mode, keep only frames from that MAC, and publish one record per frame over MQTT (QoS 0) through home Wi-Fi to a laptop hub. The hub timestamps arrival and writes one CSV per receiver. Boards are not clock-synchronized, and receivers are added purely by configuration (serial-console settings stored in flash). Each frame yields 64 subcarrier slots, of which 56 are usable; phase is discarded because carrier and sampling frequency offsets (CFO/SFO) corrupt it. Receivers do not yet check the `rx_channel_estimate_info_vld` flag. The live motion score is the mean, over subcarriers, of the 50-frame rolling standard deviation of per-frame mean-normalized amplitude. The project has three modules: a measurement platform with characterization (distance, obstruction, receiver position); presence and motion classification (empty / occupied-still / occupied-moving); and coarse 3-zone localization using several receivers, with up to 7 boards. Preliminary results: receivers log 100.1 frames/s, and in one 97 s single-link recording the median motion score was 3–4× higher while the subject moved than while they stood still.

### How sources were found, and the limits of that search

Sources were found through English-language web searches, by following citations from surveys, and by reading tool repositories, vendor documentation and GitHub issues directly. Each claim was given a verification level. Claims are **verified (primary)** when checked against the paper, abstract or tool page; **verified (metadata)** when title, venue and year were confirmed in a catalog record (dblp, DOI, arXiv, researchr) but numbers came from the abstract only; **secondary** when the figure came from a survey, press article or aggregator; and **unverified** when it came from background knowledge alone. This review cites only verified material. It labels secondary figures and preprints in the text, and it omits unverified claims or names them as unverified.

The search has real limits. **IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library and Google Scholar were not searched directly.** The publisher fetchers for MDPI, ScienceDirect, PMC and dblp were bot-blocked, so several ESP32 papers are known only from search snippets. The publication lists of the Daqing Zhang, Zheng Yang and Kun Qian groups were not searched systematically. "Wi-Dist", a system named in the project brief, could not be identified and is not cited. The multi-link search ran on a budget of about 20 search and fetch calls. Most importantly, **the full texts of the two closest prior works, Preprints.org manuscripts 202601.2378 and 202601.2295 (January 2026, not peer-reviewed), returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.** Every novelty claim in Sections 8 and 9 is therefore provisional. Before the final report, the team must read those two manuscripts and run targeted Google Scholar and IEEE Xplore searches. Suggested queries: "ESP32 CSI number of receivers", "CSI receiver placement occupancy", "RSSI CSI comparison ESP32", "ESP32-C6 CSI", "leave-one-out receiver Wi-Fi sensing".

## 2. Background: CSI records how a room bends 56 tones at once

### CSI, OFDM and multipath

802.11n transmits on orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM). The 20 MHz channel is split into 64 narrow subcarriers, about 312.5 kHz apart under the standard 802.11 numerology. Each frame begins with known training symbols (the legacy L-LTF and, for HT frames, the HT-LTF), so the receiver can estimate one complex gain per subcarrier, H(f) = |H(f)|·e^{j∠H(f)}. That vector of gains is the channel state information. Physically, H(f) is the sum of every propagation path from transmitter to receiver: the direct path, wall and furniture reflections, and reflections off people. Each path has its own attenuation and delay, and so its own phase at each subcarrier. When a person moves, the lengths of the paths they touch change, and the subcarriers interfere differently. Because the subcarriers have slightly different wavelengths, each responds a little differently to the same movement. This frequency diversity is the main reason CSI beats a single RSSI number. ESP32 tools report each subcarrier as signed 8-bit imaginary and real parts, from which amplitude is √(im² + re²) and phase is atan2(im, re) ([Hernandez & Bulut, WoWMoM 2020](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)).

On the ESP32-C6, Espressif confirmed in esp-idf issue #14271 that the HT-LTF overwrites the L-LTF in the CSI buffer. An HT20 frame therefore yields **128 bytes (64 subcarriers × 2 bytes) instead of 256**, with effective indices −28…−1 and 1…28: exactly the project's 56 usable subcarriers. Espressif also acknowledged that the published subcarrier-order documentation is inaccurate for this chip ([esp-idf #14271](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/14271)).

### Why amplitude only: phase on one free-running radio is unusable

Commodity receivers have carrier frequency offset (CFO), sampling frequency offset (SFO), symbol timing offset and PLL phase offsets, and all of them change from packet to packet. Multi-antenna systems cancel these offsets by dividing or conjugate-multiplying the CSI of two antennas on the *same* card, which share one oscillator. FarSense, for example, states that the CSI ratio of two antennas makes phase usable in a way a single antenna cannot ([Zeng et al., FarSense, IMWUT 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03994)). A single-antenna ESP32-C6 has nothing to divide by. Separate ESP32 boards each run their own oscillator, so cross-board conjugate multiplication fails too. ESPARGOS, an ESP32-based array, achieves phase coherence only by distributing a shared 40 MHz reference clock plus an on-board phase-reference frame ([Euchner & ten Brink, arXiv 2408.16377, preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.16377)). Espressif's own dual-receiver design ties two chips to one clock buffer for the same reason ([Espressif ESP-CSI solution page](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/solution-introduction/esp-csi/esp-csi-solution.html)). The project's amplitude-only design is therefore forced by physics, not just convenient, and it rules out a whole family of methods described below.

### The Fresnel-zone model: where a body can and cannot be seen

Daqing Zhang's group recast Wi-Fi sensing around Fresnel zones. These are confocal ellipses with the transmitter and receiver at the foci. The n-th boundary is the set of points where the reflected path is nλ/2 longer than the direct path (standard geometry, with λ ≈ 12.5 cm at 2.4 GHz). As a reflector crosses successive boundaries, the reflected path's phase relative to the direct path rotates by π, so the received amplitude rises and falls. Wang et al. used this model to show that whether respiration is detectable depends on the person's location and body orientation relative to the link ([Wang et al., UbiComp 2016](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/rec/conf/huc/WangZMWWWGX16.html)). The companion demo showed that **a centimetre-scale change in position** can move a subject between detectable and undetectable regions ([UbiComp 2016 demo, DOI 10.1145/2968219.2971394](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2968219.2971394)). Two consequences follow for Group 6. A single link senses a thin ellipse around its line of sight, not the whole room, which a hobbyist build also observed when it needed four boards to cover blind spots ([Hackster.io, not peer-reviewed](https://www.hackster.io/limengdu0117/esp-csi-diy-wifi-human-presence-detection-f80508)). And amplitude-only sensing has blind spots wherever the body-induced change is mostly in phase. FarSense avoids those blind spots by combining amplitude and phase from a two-antenna CSI ratio ([FarSense, arXiv 1907.03994](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03994)). The levers left to a single-antenna design are spatial diversity (more receivers at different geometries) and subcarrier diversity.

### The CSI-speed model: frequency of fluctuation encodes body speed

CARM models CSI power as a sum of static and dynamic path components. Each dynamic component oscillates at a frequency proportional to the speed at which a body part changes that path's length. A second "CSI-activity" model then ties combinations of body-part speeds to specific activities. CARM deliberately uses CSI *power* (amplitude squared) because "CSI signal power is not affected by CFO." Human motion maps to components below about 300 Hz, so CARM recommends 800 samples/s ([Wang et al., CARM, MobiCom 2015](https://old.sigmobile.org/mobicom/2015/papers/p65-wangA.pdf)). This is the theory closest to Group 6's pipeline: it is amplitude-based, so it is CFO-immune and works with one antenna. It also bounds what 100 Hz can resolve. A path-length change of 2v for a reflector moving at v = 1 m/s gives fluctuations up to about 2v/λ ≈ 16 Hz (a derivation, not a cited result), well inside the 50 Hz Nyquist limit of a 100 Hz stream. 100 Hz is therefore enough for walking-versus-still and coarse activity, but below CARM's rate for fine speed profiles. The project's motion score (rolling standard deviation of normalized amplitude) is the crudest summary of this model: it measures how much the CSI fluctuates, not at what frequency.

## 3. CSI measurement tools: ESP32 trades antennas and phase for a 100× cost cut

Before about 2019 almost every CSI paper used one of two laptop-hosted tools. Of 494 papers listed on the tools' websites, **92.5% used the Intel 5300**, 6.8% Atheros and under 1% Nexmon ([Hernandez & Bulut 2020, Table I](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)). Intel lists the 5300 as end-of-life ([Intel support](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/support/articles/000006507.html)), and a 2025 review of embedded Wi-Fi sensing describes it as legacy hardware ([Sensors 25(19):6220, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/19/6220)). Newer tools extend CSI to Broadcom chips, Wi-Fi 6 NICs and SDRs. The ESP32 lineage is the only standalone, battery-powered option under $10.

The table shows that the ESP32's real deficit is not frame rate (650/s versus about 1000/s) but **antenna count, 8-bit resolution and the absence of usable phase**. Espressif's own deployment guide anticipates Group 6's topology: a "broadcast" mode with one sender and several ESP32 receivers, which it describes only qualitatively as having the "highest detection accuracy and reliability" and for which it publishes no numbers ([espressif/esp-csi](https://github.com/espressif/esp-csi)). The ESP32-C6's Wi-Fi 6 MAC also changes how CSI is configured. On the newer MAC, CSI capture is set through `wifi_csi_acquire_config_t` (fields include `acquire_csi_ht20`, `acquire_csi_force_lltf`, `val_scale_cfg`), not the classic configuration structure ([esp-idf #14271](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/14271); [ESP-IDF vendor features, C5](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v6.0/esp32c5/api-guides/wifi-driver/wifi-vendor-features.html)). Third-party claims that the C6 can report 242 HE20 tones on recent ESP-IDF versions come from a Rust crate and a community release note and are not confirmed in Espressif documentation ([esp-csi-rs, secondary](https://docs.rs/crate/esp-csi-rs/0.13.0/source/src/lib/config/csi.rs)). They do not apply to the project's HT20 frames in any case.

## 4. Taxonomy of sensing approaches: most landmark results need hardware the ESP32-C6 lacks

### Model-based versus learning-based

Wi-Fi sensing systems split along two axes. The first is the *method*. **Model-based** systems derive features from propagation physics: Fresnel zones, the CSI-speed model, angle of arrival (AoA) and time of flight (ToF), Doppler, or power-fading models. **Learning-based** systems match CSI patterns against labeled training data, using fingerprints, dynamic time warping, SVMs, random forests or deep networks. Hybrids such as Widar 3.0 compute a physical feature and then classify it with a neural network. Model-based methods generalize better across rooms but usually need phase or several antennas. Learning-based methods run on anything, including amplitude-only data, but are tied to the room they were trained in. The second axis is the *task*: presence and motion, activity and gesture, vital signs, or localization. Within localization, systems are either device-based (the target carries a radio) or device-free (the target carries nothing, as in this project).

### Presence and motion: the task closest to solved, and to Group 6

Motion detection is the easiest task because any movement disturbs a static channel. FIMD showed that CSI is temporally stable in a static room and shows burst patterns when someone moves, and claimed better accuracy and robustness to narrowband interference than RSSI ([Xiao et al., FIMD, ICPADS 2012](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6413692)). Its accuracy figures were not retrieved for this review. FIMD is the most direct precedent for the project's motion score. Pilot added a second stage, first detecting an anomaly (presence) and then identifying location, across several transmitter-receiver pairs, and reported a large improvement over the best RSS scheme ([Xiao et al., Pilot, ICDCS 2013](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/icdcs/XiaoWYWN13.html)). Detecting someone who is *still* is much harder, as Section 7 explains. That makes the project's "occupied-still" class the hard part of Module 2.

### Activity and gesture: high in-room accuracy, much lower across rooms

E-eyes reported **over 96% true-positive and under 1% false-positive rate** for location-oriented daily activities, using CSI amplitude distributions matched by earth mover's distance and DTW. It was tested in two apartments with one AP, one occupant, no pets and a stable environment ([Wang et al., E-eyes, MobiCom 2014](https://www.winlab.rutgers.edu/~yychen/papers/E-eyes%20Device-free%20Location-oriented%20Activity%20Identification%20Using%20Fine-grained%20WiFi%20Signatures%20.pdf)). CARM reached **over 96% average accuracy** across 8 activities, and **over 80% for a new environment and a new person** it was never trained on ([CARM PDF](https://old.sigmobile.org/mobicom/2015/papers/p65-wangA.pdf)). That second number is the honest benchmark. Widar 3.0's body-coordinate velocity profile reached 92.7% in-domain and **82.6–92.4% cross-domain** without retraining, but it needs Doppler from several phase-usable links ([Zheng et al., Widar 3.0, MobiSys 2019](https://ct.cswu.me/papers/mobisys19_widar3_paper.pdf)). The pattern across the field is consistent: above 90% in the training room, and a 10–15 point drop elsewhere.

### Vital signs: Fresnel blind spots and two-antenna fixes

Respiration sensing ranges from early amplitude-periodicity work to model-based designs. Wi-Sleep (RTSS 2014) extracted breathing and posture changes from CSI ([PolyU record](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/wi-sleep-contactless-sleep-monitoring-via-wifi-signals/)). Liu et al. tracked breathing and heart rate during sleep with one device-AP pair ([MobiHoc 2015 PDF](https://mosis.eecs.utk.edu/publications/liu2015tracking.pdf)); the error figures commonly quoted for it come from surveys and are not repeated here. FarSense reached about **8 m range, more than double prior work**, but only by using the two-antenna CSI ratio ([arXiv 1907.03994](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03994)).

### Localization: decimetres with phase and antennas, metres without

Device-based systems such as SpotFi (**40 cm median error** with three antennas per AP and joint AoA-ToF super-resolution) and Chronos (**65 cm LOS / 98 cm NLOS median** from one AP, by stitching ToF across bands) are landmarks. Both depend on phase ([SpotFi, SIGCOMM 2015](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/CS598WSI/sp2021/Papers%5CSpotFi_SIGCOMM15.pdf); [Chronos, NSDI 2016](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/CS598WSI/sp2021/Papers%5CChronos_NSDI16.pdf)). Device-free CSI localization is closer to this project. MonoPHY reported **1.36 m median error** from a single AP-laptop pair ([Abdel-Nasser et al., WCNC 2013](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/wcnc/Abdel-NasserSSY13)). LiFS reached about **0.5 m LOS / 1.1 m NLOS median** by selecting subcarriers that multipath barely affects, across multiple links, with low calibration effort ([Wang et al., LiFS, MobiCom 2016](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1527423/1/LiFS%20Final%20Submission%20Manuscript.pdf)). LiFS's subcarrier-power approach is in principle compatible with amplitude-only data.

## 5. ESP32-specific work: active, mostly single-room, and silent on the C6

### The tool lineage and the edge-computing thread

Hernandez and Bulut's ESP32 CSI Tool is the canonical citation for using the ESP32 as a standalone CSI sensor. It collects CSI as an AP or station, logs to serial or SD card, and runs on battery, in about 1,000 lines of ESP-IDF code ([WoWMoM 2020](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)). Its key experimental finding is directly relevant to this project: letting the receiver be repositioned or move gave DNN activity-recognition gains of "upwards of 29.4% and 28.2%" over a static receiver. **Receiver placement is a first-order variable on ESP32 hardware.** The same group's IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials paper (vol. 25, no. 1, pp. 46–76, 2023) surveys signal processing for edge sensing and measures per-method computation time on board an ESP32 ([researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/HernandezB23/bibtex)). Later work includes one-sided through-wall occupancy monitoring ([ICC 2021](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/ICC21_Adversarial_Sensing.pdf)), federated learning across ESP32 clients ([IEEE IoT-J 2022](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/FL-IoT-journal-2021.pdf)), and a dissertation on scalable on-device sensing ([VCU 2023](https://scholarscompass.vcu.edu/etd/7268/)). The group frames scaling as a *distributed-learning* problem, not as adding receivers to one sensing network.

### Peer-reviewed ESP32 applications

The strongest peer-reviewed ESP32 result is long-range through-wall activity recognition. Strohmayer and Kampel evaluated four ESP32-S3 directional antenna systems in an 18 m office spanning five rooms. Using EfficientNetV2 on amplitude spectrograms, they reached **92.0 ± 3.5% NLOS accuracy with a biquad antenna and 86.8 ± 4.7% with the PIFA plus a reflector**, and released the Wallhack1.8k dataset of 1,806 spectrograms ([arXiv 2401.01388, published at ICIP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01388)). The roughly 5-point gap between antennas supports Espressif's advice that an external antenna beats the PCB antenna ([esp-csi](https://github.com/espressif/esp-csi)). An earlier ICVS 2023 paper from the same authors covers through-wall HAR with the ESP32 ([TU Wien repositum](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/190653)). Wong et al. published a multi-transceiver ESP32-S3 dataset: four boards, 10 activities, 6 participants, in a controlled lab ([Data in Brief 55, 2024](https://doaj.org/article/a4475ad316fd4daf8e478d3076223dda); details from snippets). Macedo et al. built a four-ESP32 localization testbed with fixed receivers and **removed one ESP at a time to measure each receiver's contribution**. This is the closest peer-reviewed precedent for a receiver ablation, though its accuracy numbers and synchronization method could not be read ([Future Internet 17(9):395, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-5903/17/9/395)). VitalCSI estimated respiratory rate from two ESP32s using amplitude CSI and found accuracy degrades below 12 breaths/min ([Sensors 2026, DOI 10.3390/s26010225](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26010225); authors not retrieved). A *Computers in Biology and Medicine* (2023) respiration study reports 92.3% breaths-per-minute accuracy from raw ESP32 CSI. Those figures come from a search snippet, and the authors and title were not retrieved ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010482523012908)).

### Preprints that sit closest to this project

Three recent, **non-peer-reviewed** manuscripts are the nearest neighbours to Group 6, and all three need direct reading. The first, "Device-Free Indoor Localization with ESP32 Wi-Fi CSI Fingerprints… Multi-Link Experiments" (Preprints.org 202601.2378, January 2026), reportedly uses three ESP32 transceivers around a 6 × 4 m lab, CSI at 100 Hz, 35 grid points and over 1.2 million snapshots, and reaches sub-meter median error with three links. It asserts, citing literature rather than its own test, that ESP32 RSSI localization suffers meter-level errors ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2378)). Its authors, topology and any link ablation are unverified. The second, Chaudhari et al., "Room-Level Occupancy Estimation via Wi-Fi CSI on ESP32 Nodes" (Preprints.org 202601.2295, 29 January 2026), used one ESP32-WROOM-32 AP/STA link per zone across 4 zones and about 250 h of data. It reports **zone-averaged F1 of 0.96 (binary) and 0.89 (0 / 1 / 2+ people)** with gradient boosting, and reportedly lists single-link-per-zone reliance as a limitation ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2295); limitation not confirmed from full text). The third, Khamaisi and Rodrigues, "Less is More: The Dilution Effect in Multi-Link Wireless Sensing" (arXiv 2602.10823, 2026), ran a 9-node, 72-link ESP32-C3 mesh in a residence for 12 days. **One well-placed link beat the full mesh (AUC 0.541 vs 0.489)**, random link selection matched optimized selection, and placement mattered 2.7× more than classifier choice. The authors attribute this to links whose Fresnel zones miss activity areas adding noise, and they released 312 hours of labeled CSI ([arXiv 2602.10823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10823)). Both AUCs are near chance (0.5), so the result shows a hard task more than a clean scaling law. It is still a direct warning against assuming that more receivers help.

### Phase-coherent ESP32 arrays mark the other end of the design space

ESPARGOS uses eight ESP32-S2 chips sharing a 40 MHz reference clock and a PCB-distributed reference frame to make CSI phase-coherent across chips. Its target applications include activity recognition, presence detection, localization and channel charting, and it publishes labeled datasets ([arXiv 2502.09405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09405); [arXiv 2408.16377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16377); both preprints, peer-reviewed venues not confirmed). It shows that phase-based ESP32 sensing is possible, but only with dedicated clock-distribution hardware. Group 6's free-running boards cannot do this, and that is a reason to measure what *unsynchronized* amplitude networks can do.

### Hobbyist projects: design patterns, not evidence

Community projects demonstrate interest and useful design patterns but carry no peer review. RuView claims presence, vital signs and pose from ESP32-S3 meshes, lists the C6 as "pending validation", and reports in its own README a held-out presence accuracy of 82.3% (after retracting an earlier "100%" that came from single-class data) and live CSI-only pose of only 3.0% PCK@20 ([GitHub RuView, not peer-reviewed](https://github.com/ruvnet/RuView)). Other examples are a Home Assistant presence build ([forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/human-presence-using-wifi-sensing-csi-on-esp32/791452)) and Espressif's `wifi_sensing_demo` with on-site training ([esp-csi](https://github.com/espressif/esp-csi)). This review cites them only for architecture, never for accuracy.

### What is missing

The search found **no peer-reviewed ESP32-C6 CSI sensing paper**. The C6 appears only in vendor documentation, GitHub issues and hobby projects. It also found no head-to-head ESP32 versus Intel 5300 benchmark on the same task and room; no ESP32-specific measurement of CFO, AGC steps, packet loss or inter-board clock drift; and no published measurement of MQTT backhaul jitter for CSI streaming.

## 6. Multi-link device-free localization: two decades of meshes, almost no stars

### Radio tomographic imaging established the multi-link idea with RSS

Device-free passive localization began with Youssef, Mah and Agrawala, who showed it was feasible with ordinary Wi-Fi equipment ([MobiCom 2007](https://www.csd.uoc.gr/~hy439/reading/list_2011/10.1.1.112.6849.pdf)). Wilson and Patwari formalized it as **radio tomographic imaging (RTI)**. Each link's RSS attenuation is modelled as a weighted sum of attenuations in voxels inside an ellipse around the link (the same geometry as the first Fresnel zone), and an image is reconstructed by regularized inversion. Their testbed used **28 TelosB 2.4 GHz nodes** around a 14 ft square, with every node pair acting as a link, and they argued that imaging power grows "as Metcalf's Law with the number of nodes" ([Wilson & Patwari, IEEE TMC 2010; 2008 preprint read](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)). Variance-based RTI imaged the windowed *variance* of RSS rather than mean attenuation, and tracked people through walls with a 34-node network over 780 sq ft ([arXiv 0909.5417, published IEEE TMC 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5417)). Its accuracy, "about three feet", comes from press coverage, not the paper ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2009-10-wi-fi-walls.amp)). **Variance-based RTI is the direct RSS ancestor of Group 6's rolling-standard-deviation motion score**, and it shares the same weakness: a still person creates little variance.

The Utah group then addressed that weakness. Zhao et al. compared the current RSS histogram against an empty-room calibration histogram using a **kernel distance**, which localized stationary as well as moving people and beat earlier methods in five NLOS experiments ([IPSN 2013](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2461381.2461410)). Wilson and Patwari's skew-Laplace fade-level model likewise localized stationary people by modelling how RSS changes depend on position and on each link's fade level ([IEEE TMC 2012](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tmc/WilsonP12.html)). Kaltiokallio, Bocca and Patwari showed that choosing channels by fade level beats choosing them by packet reception rate. In their words, "a link's fade level is more important than a link's communication performance in channel selection" ([MASS 2012](https://typeset.io/pdf/enhancing-the-accuracy-of-radio-tomographic-imaging-using-4wqgx4619v.pdf)). Their "Follow @grandma" deployment produced room-level, area-of-interest outputs in a real home over the long term ([SenseApp 2012](https://typeset.io/pdf/follow-grandma-long-term-device-free-localization-for-5ghkxhgm60.pdf)). This is the closest precedent for zone output rather than coordinates.

### CSI multi-link work added subcarriers, but on multi-antenna NICs

CSI-based device-free localization (Pilot, LiFS) generalized RTI's insight to the subcarrier level. LiFS's selection of subcarriers that multipath barely affects is the CSI analogue of RTI's fade-level channel selection ([LiFS manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1527423/1/LiFS%20Final%20Submission%20Manuscript.pdf)). Each ESP32 frame delivers 56 subcarrier amplitudes at once, a frequency-diversity analogue of multi-channel RTI that needs no channel hopping. In this literature, though, "multi-link" means several AP-client pairs on Intel or Atheros cards, usually with phase available. A CNN-based study notes that multi-target device-free localization remains a major open challenge ([CoMuTe, arXiv 2003.05734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05734)).

### Unsynchronized receivers are aligned by sequence numbers, not clocks

Low-cost multi-receiver systems avoid clock synchronization altogether. A 2026 ESP32-S3 gait study reportedly used one transmitter and three receivers over USB, and **matched frames across receivers by a packet sequence number** ([arXiv 2601.02177, preprint; details from a search summary only](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02177)). ESPARGOS clusters frames after reception and accepts that receivers miss frames ([arXiv 2502.09405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09405)). No source found quantifies how much timing misalignment amplitude-feature fusion can tolerate.

### Star versus mesh: why Group 6's topology is different

RTI meshes use every node pair, so N nodes give N(N−1)/2 links, and adding one node adds N−1 links at once. An 8-node RTI study notes that prior work typically used more than 20 nodes ([ECTI-EEC paper; title and authors not extracted](https://ph02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/ECTI-EEC/article/download/249427/170779/941282)). Group 6's star (1 broadcaster, up to 6 receivers) gives only N links, far too few for tomographic image reconstruction. But it has a property meshes lack: **each added receiver contributes exactly one new link geometry, and costs only a configuration change.** That makes clean accuracy-versus-N curves cheap to produce, which mesh studies cannot isolate. No paper found reports such a curve for CSI on low-cost hardware.

## 7. Open challenges: generalization, stillness and receiver effects dominate

**Domain shift is the field's central unsolved problem.** CSI records the whole multipath channel, so a model trained in one room, position, person or device degrades elsewhere. Chen, Zhou and Lin's cross-domain survey sorts solutions into five families: domain-invariant features, virtual sample generation, transfer learning, few-shot learning and big-data approaches ([ACM CSUR 55(11), 2023](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/cross-domain-wifi-sensing-with-channel-state-information-a-survey/831481475873374208-2425)). A 2025 survey of more than 200 studies names **device heterogeneity, human body diversity and environmental diversity** as the three root causes ([arXiv 2503.08008, preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08008v1)). The flagship fix, Widar 3.0's BVP, needs phase from several links. The hardware-agnostic options (EI-style adversarial training, WiAG-style virtual samples, transfer learning) need data from at least two layouts. The search found no verified leave-one-environment-out result on single-antenna amplitude-only hardware.

**Stationary presence is much harder than motion.** A still person shows up only through millimetre-scale breathing or a static change in the multipath profile, and an adaptive baseline can gradually absorb that change and "learn away" the person. Respiration detectability depends on centimetre-scale position relative to Fresnel zones ([UbiComp 2016 demo](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2968219.2971394)). The published blind-spot fix needs two antennas ([FarSense](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03994)). RSS radio tomography solved stationary localization with histogram or kernel distances to an empty baseline ([Zhao et al., IPSN 2013](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2461381.2461410)), but the search found no application of that idea to multi-link ESP32 CSI amplitude.

**Receiver hardware effects, especially AGC, are a hidden domain.** Portner, Gringoli, Hollick and Asadi fed identical signals to several commercial receivers. They found **automatic gain control to be the dominant source of cross-device CSI differences**, and showed that a lightweight gain-alignment step recovers up to 75% of the accuracy lost in cross-device activity recognition ([arXiv 2605.26836, listed as IEEE IoT-J 13(10), May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26836)). Espressif ships an `esp_csi_gain_ctrl` component that compensates AGC and FFT gain against a baseline ([component registry](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp_csi_gain_ctrl/versions/0.1.5/readme)); whether it supports the C6 was not checked. Another ESP32-S3 study multiplies each subcarrier by the reciprocal of the per-packet gain ([CiUAV, arXiv 2505.21216, preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21216)). Community forum posts dispute how much ESP32 amplitude is actually distorted ([esp32.com](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=112486)). In a network of nominally identical receivers, cross-*board* shift may matter as much as cross-room shift.

**Sampling is irregular and lossy.** CSI exists only when a frame arrives, so the sample rate is the received packet rate. Most academic systems inject traffic at hundreds of hertz to get uniform sampling. IEEE 802.11bf-2025, approved on 28 May 2025 and published on 26 September 2025, standardizes how sensing measurements are requested, scheduled and reported, but not the algorithms ([ANSI webstore](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/ieee/ieee80211bf2025); [Du et al., IEEE COMST 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17661)). The ESP32-C6 predates 11bf and relies on vendor-specific CSI callbacks.

**Multi-node fusion, benchmarks and privacy remain open.** Independent clocks rule out cross-node phase and Doppler fusion. Sensing traffic competes with data traffic for airtime. CrossSense's gains were reported only in single-link settings ([Lancaster eprint](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/126425)), so multi-link generalization is largely unmeasured. SenseFi created a unified deep-learning benchmark on NTU-Fi, UT-HAR and Widar data ([Yang et al., Patterns 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07859v3)), but those datasets come from multi-antenna NICs with different subcarrier counts and do not transfer to 56-subcarrier ESP32 amplitude. On privacy, IRShield showed that passive eavesdroppers can infer motion "using simple statistical methods" and cut that detection to at most 5% with intelligent reflecting surfaces ([IEEE S&P 2022, arXiv 2112.01967](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01967)). This sits uneasily with surveys that present Wi-Fi sensing as privacy-friendlier than cameras ([Tan et al., IEEE IoT-J 2022](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10473796-commodity-wifi-sensing-ten-years-status-challenges-opportunities)).

## 9. Proposed original contributions: a receiver-scaling ablation leads the ranking

The candidates below are ranked by a combination of novelty (how clearly the gap is open, given the limited search) and feasibility (fit with the hardware and the 7 December demo). Every novelty claim is **provisional** until the team reads Preprints.org 202601.2378 and 202601.2295 and runs the Scholar and IEEE Xplore queries listed in Section 1. If either preprint already reports accuracy against number of links, C1 loses most of its novelty and C2 becomes the lead.

### C1 (rank 1). Accuracy versus receiver count, with leave-one-receiver-out robustness

**Gap filled:** G1. **Closest prior work and how this differs:** The ESP32 fingerprinting preprint used three links, apparently at a fixed count ([202601.2378](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2378)). The occupancy preprint used one link per zone ([202601.2295](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2295)). Macedo et al. removed one receiver at a time from a four-ESP32 testbed ([Future Internet 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-5903/17/9/395)). The dilution study varied links in a 72-link *mesh* and reported near-chance AUCs ([arXiv 2602.10823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10823)). RTI link selection worked in dense meshes for imaging ([MASS 2012](https://typeset.io/pdf/enhancing-the-accuracy-of-radio-tomographic-imaging-using-4wqgx4619v.pdf)). C1 differs by evaluating **every subset** of up to 6 receivers in a **one-broadcaster star** (all 63 non-empty subsets), for **both** 3-state presence and 3-zone localization. It reports the full curve, the minimum N for a target accuracy, and a greedy placement ranking. **Experiment:** 1 TX and 6 RX placed around one room divided into three zones. Seven conditions: empty; still in zone A, B or C; walking in zone A, B or C. Each condition is recorded for 2 min, twice, per session, giving about 30 min per session. Run at least 4 sessions on different days, with at least 2 subjects if possible. Use 2 s windows. Features per receiver: motion score, per-subcarrier normalized-amplitude variance, and mean and spread of amplitude. Use a simple classifier (logistic regression or random forest) so that geometry rather than model capacity drives the results. **Metrics:** macro-F1 and accuracy under **leave-one-session-out** cross-validation (so windows from the same recording never appear in both train and test), with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. Report per-N mean and spread across subsets, the best and worst subset at each N, and the greedy-selection order. **Expected outcome:** zone accuracy rises steeply from N = 1 to N = 3 (one link cannot tell zones apart that are equidistant from its ellipse) and then plateaus or dips. Placement explains more variance than N, consistent with the dilution study. A plateau or dip is still a publishable finding. **Risks:** a preprint has already done this; three zones are too easy (ceiling effect) or too hard in one room; MQTT loss differs by receiver and confounds N. Mitigate the last by reporting per-receiver loss and by using counter-aligned windows. **Effort:** about 1 week of data collection (shared with C2–C4) and 3–4 days of analysis. This is the cheapest contribution because it is offline subsampling of one dataset.

### C2 (rank 2). RSSI versus CSI amplitude from the same frames, same receivers, same fusion

**Gap filled:** G2. **Closest prior work and how this differs:** FIMD and Pilot claimed CSI beats RSS on Intel NICs ([FIMD](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6413692); [Pilot](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/icdcs/XiaoWYWN13.html)). The ESP32 preprint contrasts with RSSI only by citing literature ([202601.2378](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.2378)). The entire RTI literature is RSS-only ([RTI](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf)). C2 makes the comparison **within one frame stream**, so hardware, placement, timing and labels are identical and only the feature set changes. It is also crossed with receiver count, giving RSSI-only, CSI-only and combined curves against N. This connects the RTI and CSI literatures directly. **Experiment:** the same dataset as C1, with RSSI and noise floor logged per frame. Train three feature sets with the same classifier and windows. **Metrics:** macro-F1 difference (CSI − RSSI) with paired bootstrap confidence intervals per N and per task, and separately for the still-versus-empty sub-problem. **Expected outcome:** CSI clearly beats RSSI for presence and stillness with few receivers, because 56 subcarriers give frequency diversity that one RSSI number lacks. The gap narrows as N grows, because spatial diversity substitutes for frequency diversity. If the gap does *not* narrow, that is a useful negative result for anyone choosing between cheap RSSI networks and CSI. **Risks:** ESP32 RSSI is quantized to whole dB and may be uninformative at 100 Hz, giving a trivial win; report RSSI variance features to be fair. **Effort:** about 2 days on top of C1, provided RSSI is logged from day one.

### C3 (rank 3). Multi-link stillness detection with an empty-room distribution distance

**Gap filled:** G3, and it strengthens Module 2's hardest class. **Closest prior work and how this differs:** kernel-distance RTI localized stationary people with RSS on dense 802.15.4 meshes ([Zhao et al., IPSN 2013](https://www.doi.org/10.1145/2461381.2461410)). FarSense handled respiration blind spots with two antennas ([FarSense](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03994)). Pilot detected presence anomalies on multi-antenna NICs ([Pilot](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/icdcs/XiaoWYWN13.html)). C3 adapts the histogram or kernel distance to **per-subcarrier amplitude** on a single-antenna star, and measures **how many receivers stillness detection needs**. **Experiment:** from the C1 dataset, add an empty-room baseline recorded just before each session (5 min). For each window and receiver, compute the distance between the current per-subcarrier amplitude distribution and the baseline: a kernel distance, Jensen-Shannon divergence, or simply the shift in mean normalized amplitude. Compare this with the variance-based motion score on empty versus still. **Metrics:** ROC AUC and F1 for empty versus still, per N, plus the time a still subject remains detectable when the baseline is re-estimated (to expose the "learning away" failure). **Expected outcome:** variance alone performs near chance on empty versus still, given that the project's preliminary 3–4× ratio is motion versus still, not still versus empty. The distance feature lifts it substantially, but only when at least one receiver's link passes near the subject. **Risks:** baseline drift over a session (doors, temperature, people in adjacent rooms) masquerades as presence; subjects fidget. Use a seated, phone-down protocol and record drift with repeated empty segments. **Effort:** 2–3 days of analysis plus 5 min per session of extra recording.

### C4 (rank 4). Timing-tolerance curve for fusing unsynchronized receivers

**Gap filled:** G4. **Closest prior work and how this differs:** sequence-number alignment is used without a tolerance study ([arXiv 2601.02177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02177)), and ESPARGOS clusters frames after reception ([arXiv 2502.09405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09405)). C4 measures directly how accuracy degrades as receiver streams are misaligned. **Experiment:** align all receivers by the TX counter (ground truth). Then inject artificial offsets of 0, 10, 50, 100, 250, 500 and 1000 ms into some receivers' streams and re-run the C1 classifier. Separately, measure real hub-arrival-time skew against counter alignment to show how large MQTT jitter actually is. **Metrics:** macro-F1 against offset; distribution of hub-arrival skew across receivers. **Expected outcome:** window-level features tolerate tens to low hundreds of milliseconds (based on the 2 s windows and the motion timescales derived in Section 2), so hub timestamps are probably adequate for features but not for frame-level cross-receiver correlation. **Risks:** a small result; frame it as a methodological side-finding. **Effort:** 1–2 days, but it requires the counter fix.

### C5 (rank 5, stretch). Star versus rotating-transmitter pseudo-mesh on the same seven boards

**Gap filled:** G1 extended to topology. **Closest prior work and how this differs:** all RTI work uses meshes ([RTI](https://span.ece.utah.edu/uploads/RTI.pdf); [VRTI](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5417)), and the ESP32-C3 dilution study used a mesh ([arXiv 2602.10823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10823)). No same-hardware star-versus-mesh comparison was found. **Experiment:** in some sessions, rotate which board transmits, building up more link geometries from the same boards. Compare accuracy at matched link counts. **Risk:** requires firmware so any board can take the transmitter role, and multiplies session time. **Effort:** 1–1.5 weeks; attempt only if C1–C4 are done early.

## 10. Practical implications for the current system

The literature implies several low-cost changes to the firmware and hub, and most of them are prerequisites for the contributions above. First, **record the transmitter's payload counter in every receiver record**, alongside the 802.11 sequence-control field if exposed. The counter already exists in the 4-byte payload. It gives exact frame-level alignment across receivers with no clock sync, the method other multi-receiver ESP32 systems use ([arXiv 2601.02177](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02177)), and it turns gaps into exact per-receiver loss counts. Hub arrival time adds Wi-Fi and broker jitter and should be kept only as a diagnostic. Second, **drop frames whose `rx_channel_estimate_info_vld` flag is false**. Espressif confirmed that invalid CSI can reach the callback on the C6, and users saw zeros and malformed samples ([esp-idf #14271](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/14271)). Count the dropped frames rather than hiding them. Third, **configure CSI explicitly through `wifi_csi_acquire_config_t`** (HT20 on, L-LTF forcing off), and write the subcarrier index map used into the CSV header. The C6 layout departs from the documentation, so the 56 effective indices (−28…−1, 1…28) should be checked empirically, for example by confirming that the null and guard positions are consistently near zero ([#14271](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/issues/14271)).

Fourth, **log RSSI, noise floor and the receiver's local timestamp with every frame**. The esp-csi repository documents that each packet carries RSSI, noise floor and reception time ([espressif/esp-csi](https://github.com/espressif/esp-csi)). RSSI makes contribution C2 free, and local timestamps let the team measure on-board timing jitter separately from MQTT jitter. Fifth, **deal with AGC**, because a gain step looks like motion to an amplitude-only detector. AGC is the dominant cross-receiver effect ([Portner et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26836)). The current per-frame mean normalization already cancels a common gain factor across subcarriers, which is a point in the design's favour. On top of that, log any per-packet gain fields the C6 API exposes, test whether `esp_csi_gain_ctrl` supports the C6 ([component](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp_csi_gain_ctrl/versions/0.1.5/readme)), and run one Module 1 test that swaps boards between positions to measure inter-board differences.

Sixth, **treat sampling as irregular**. Resample each receiver onto the counter grid, interpolating or masking gaps, and report the loss rate per receiver and session. Expect MQTT QoS 0 over a shared 2.4 GHz channel to lose more frames as receivers are added. ESP-NOW frames and MQTT uplinks share the home AP's channel, so sensing and backhaul compete for airtime, a contention effect the search found no ESP32 measurement of. Seventh, **match the evaluation to the claim.** The preliminary 3–4× motion-to-still ratio comes from one 97 s recording and is not evidence of generalization. Report results under leave-one-session-out validation with confidence intervals, and state explicitly that the still-versus-empty contrast is the hard case. Eighth, **record metadata**: board-to-position mapping, antenna orientation, door states, people in adjacent rooms. Placement matters more than the classifier ([arXiv 2602.10823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10823)), and receiver repositioning moved ESP32 accuracy by up to about 29% ([WoWMoM 2020](https://ebulutvcu.github.io/wowmom20-WiFiSensing.pdf)). Finally, **minimize privacy exposure**. Enable TLS and authentication on the MQTT broker, keep raw CSI on the local network, and note in the report that passive CSI can reveal presence and routine to a third party ([IRShield](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01967)).

## Conclusion

This review changes the project's question. Group 6 started from "how accurately can cheap boards detect and locate people?", a question the literature has answered many times on better hardware and in rooms the project will never reproduce. The more useful question is "what does each additional cheap, unsynchronized receiver actually buy, and is CSI amplitude worth its cost over RSSI?". Two decades of RTI asserted that more nodes help. A 2026 ESP32 mesh study suggests they can hurt. No source found has measured the answer for a one-broadcaster star, the topology Espressif itself recommends. The project's configuration-only receivers make that measurement unusually cheap, so a limitation (no phase, no antennas, no sync) becomes a clean experimental knob.

Two cautions should shape the final report. First, the contribution is a measurement result, not an algorithm, and its novelty is only as strong as a search that skipped IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library and Google Scholar and could not open the two closest preprints. Re-checking both is week-one work, not an afterthought. Second, the field's hardest problems (stillness, cross-room transfer, receiver gain effects) are exactly where amplitude-only hardware is weakest. A result showing where four to six ESP32-C6 receivers stop helping, or where RSSI is enough, is as valuable as a high accuracy number, and more honest.
